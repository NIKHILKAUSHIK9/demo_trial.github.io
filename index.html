StarNest

Gaia DR3 Pipeline

Photometric & Kinematic Study of Open Star Clusters

Open clusters are among the best natural laboratories for stellar evolution: their stars formed together, share a common age and chemical composition, and lie at nearly the same distance. Turning raw survey data into cluster parameters, however, normally means stitching together separate scripts for quality cuts, membership selection, extinction correction, isochrone fitting and mass-function analysis.

This application automates that full workflow for Gaia DR3 data and accompanies our study of six open clusters (Haffner 14, Melotte 111, NGC 2910, Ruprecht 147, UBC 52, UPK 545) spanning a wide range of log(age/yr).

What the pipeline does

Six stages from raw Gaia DR3 catalogue to mass function — hover (or tap) a stage for details

01

Quality filtering

Clean Gaia astrometry

RUWE, astrometric excess noise and goodness-of-fit, visibility periods, parallax S/N and proper-motion error cuts.

Open
02

Membership

Who belongs to the cluster?

A two-component Gaussian Mixture Model in (μα, μδ, ϖ) space, with a distance pre-selection window and membership probabilities (members: P ≥ 0.8).

Open
03

Photometry

Distance, extinction, CMD

Distance and extinction correction (E(B−V) from IRSA dust maps, Gaia band coefficients), then CMD construction with PARSEC isochrone fitting for age and metallicity.

Open
04

Evolutionary stage labelling

Where each star is in its life

Main sequence, pre-main sequence, red giant/supergiant and white dwarf candidates.

Open
05

Kinematics

How the cluster moves

Proper-motion vectors and spatial distribution of members in heliocentric and Galactocentric frames.

Open
06

Initial Mass Function

How the masses are distributed

Log-binned mass functions with Poisson errors, power-law slope Γ compared against Salpeter (1955), Kroupa (2001) and Chabrier (2003).

Open

How to use it

Choose a cluster in the sidebar, adjust the filter and membership thresholds, and inspect the vector-point diagram, CMD, spatial map and mass function as they update.

Caveats

Ages of clusters without a clear main-sequence turn-off are uncertain, and mass-function slopes for low-N clusters are consistent with, not tight constraints on, the Salpeter value. Results are exploratory and not a substitute for detailed follow-up.
